Maud Pie and Mudbriar rode back to Canterlot on the special commissioner’s train alongside the visiting Imperial officials. From the moment they boarded, several Imperial agents in black uniforms stared unblinkingly at their every move. Maud Pie, however, paid them no mind and simply gazed out at the swirling snow. Her breath fogged the glass, tracing a thin veil over the grand buildings becoming clearer on the mountainside below.

Leaning her head on Mudbriar’s shoulder, she asked softly, “Once Father and Mother recover, will you marry me?”
Mudbriar cleared his throat and, with solemn deliberation, replied, “After rigorous analysis from geopolitical dynamics to neurocircuit psychology, although proposing to you now would impose substantial short‑term costs on the quarry farm, this action is not only macroscopically feasible but also promises positive shifts in irrational emotions and biological well‑being. …”

Maud Pie closed her eyes, smiling as she listened to Mudbriar’s earnest, cascading arguments. Outside, the wind’s howl faded to a gentle whisper.
