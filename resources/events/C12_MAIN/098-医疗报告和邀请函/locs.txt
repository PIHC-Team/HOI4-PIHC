It had been quite some time since the airship’s visit, and no matter how hard the Pie sisters pressed for news of their parents, the Empire remained silent—no word was ever passed along. Their worries deepened like perlite sinking into still water. At dawn one morning, an imperial special train glided quietly into the quarry station. A squad of imperial envoys, cloaked in black, leapt down and hurried toward City Hall.

An hour later, a slim, rectangular package lay on the round table. Maud Pie and Limestone Pie exchanged glances, but neither made to open it.
“What do you suppose is inside?” Maud asked.
“This isn’t the Empress’s usual style,” Limestone replied. “If the Empire truly returned, they wouldn’t want outsiders to know.” She watched Maud unfurl the package as if inspecting a curious stone. “It can’t be anything good.”

Limestone’s voice dropped low, laced with a simmering anger—both sisters understood what lay behind their dread: the fate of their parents. Steeling herself, Limestone carefully cut open the package and read aloud:

    To Limestone Pie and the loyal members of the Pie family:
    Since the dawn of the New Era, our quarry farms have stood as paragons of imperial autonomy—nurturing the prosperity of all ponies and laying the groundwork for greatness. Your family’s renowned filial piety moves me deeply. Though I reign from the highest throne, there is much to admire in your example. Hence, with genuine goodwill, I have arranged care to relieve your parents’ suffering.

    Good news: Your parents have turned from the brink of death and are now under the diligent watch of professional healers. They eagerly await reunion with you.

    By imperial decree,
    Empress Cozy Glow

Maud folded the slender invitation shut. The other documents—medical reports for Igneous Rock Pie and Cloudy Quartz—were strewn on the table. Limestone flipped through them, brow furrowed. Unfamiliar medical jargon and cryptic data leapt from the pages, mingled with odd wafts of lavender and charred sage. A bright-red imperial seal stamped each report, as if to guarantee its authenticity.

Once Maud finished reading, she gathered the papers with care and placed them back on the table. Seeing Maud remain calm, Limestone’s nerves snapped—she swept the envelopes and reports into the air, letting them flutter wildly around the room.

“Have you seen the news, Maud? Listen: I don’t know what the Empress is plotting. That blasted airship… Father, Mother—they might already be… and now she’s dangling this as a trap!”

Her voice cracked, raw with emotion. At the mention of their parents, Limestone’s anger gave way to tears and trembling words. “But… what if this is true? We need certainty before our next step. We can’t cling to false hope and lose our chance at the right decision.”

Taking a steadying breath, Maud bent to pick up the scattered papers. “Are you mad? It’s obviously a scheme by those tyrants. I’m leaving for Appleloosa to warn Braeburn—we’ll ready ourselves for war against the Empire.”

Limestone couldn’t hold back any longer. With a crash, she shoved open the door and turned to go, tears streaming down her cheeks. She covered her face with her mane and sank to the floor, chastened by her own harshness.

Slowly, Maud knelt and drew Limestone into a tight embrace—perhaps the last one they’d share. After a moment, Limestone whispered through her sobs, “I’m sorry… I can’t lose you too.”
